# BitClock privacy policy

*Last updated: 30 October 2026*

BitClock is an alarm clock, timer, stopwatch, clock and notes app for Android and iPhone, made by the BitClock developer ("we"). This policy explains what happens with your data when you use BitClock.

**The short version:** BitClock has **no accounts, no ads, no analytics and no tracking**. Your alarms, notes, timers and settings stay **on your phone**. A few optional features fetch information from the internet, and only send what they need for that.

## 1. Data that stays on your phone

These are stored only on your device, in the app's private storage, and are never sent to us:

- alarms, their sounds and settings, and the wake-up briefing options
- your news choices: the news service, sections, interests and city
- your wake-up streak (how many alarms in a row you got up at the first ring)
- notes and checklists
- timers, presets, cooking timers and stopwatch sessions
- world clock cities, the weather city you chose, your language, theme and time format
- your birth date, if you entered one for the horoscope, and your own luck messages
- sound files you picked for alarms (a copy is kept inside the app)
- your BitClock Pro status

Uninstalling the app deletes all of them. **Backups** (Settings → Backup → Export data) are files you create yourself and keep wherever you choose; we never see them.

## 2. Permissions, and what they are used for

The first time you open BitClock, a short guide asks for these one at a time and explains each one. You can open it again in **Settings → Permission guide**.

| Permission | Used for | Leaves your phone? |
|---|---|---|
| Alarms and reminders, notifications, full-screen notifications, playing sound, vibration, starting after a restart | Ringing alarms and timers on time, also over the lock screen and after a reboot | No |
| **Approximate location** (optional) | Finding your city for local news headlines | Only the city or country name, in the news request (see section 3) |
| **Calendar** (optional, *Today's calendar* in the briefing) | Showing and reading today's events after an alarm | No. Events are read on the phone at that moment and not stored. |
| **Notification access** (optional, *Missed notifications* in the briefing, Android only) | Counting which apps sent notifications during the night, and reading the count aloud | No. Only the app names, the count and one title are used, on the phone; message texts are never read or stored. |
| Internet | The optional online features in section 3, and *Contact us* | See section 3 |

You can refuse or withdraw any optional permission in your phone's settings; the rest of the app keeps working.

## 3. Optional features that use the internet

They only run when you switch them on, and they connect straight from your phone to the service; there is no BitClock server in between. These services receive your device's IP address, as every website does, and process it under their own privacy policies.

| Feature | Service | What is sent |
|---|---|---|
| Weather | [Open-Meteo](https://open-meteo.com) | The coordinates of the city you picked; the search text when you look for a city |
| News in the wake-up briefing | Google News ([privacy policy](https://policies.google.com/privacy)), or the service you chose: BBC News ([privacy policy](https://www.bbc.co.uk/usingthebbc/privacy/)), Euronews ([privacy policy](https://www.euronews.com/terms-and-conditions#privacypolicy)) or DW ([privacy policy](https://www.dw.com/en/european-union-general-data-protection-regulation-gdpr-valid-may-25-2018/a-63500655)) | The language; for Google News also the sections and interests you chose, and your country or city for local news |
| *On this day*, born and died today, *Trending today* | Wikimedia / Wikipedia ([privacy policy](https://foundation.wikimedia.org/wiki/Policy:Privacy_policy)) | The date and the language |
| *Contact us* | [Web3Forms](https://web3forms.com) (forwards the message to our inbox) | Your name, your e-mail address, your question and the app version, **only when you press Send** |

A headline you tap opens in your browser, on the news site, which then applies its own privacy policy. BitClock doesn't learn which stories you read.

## 4. Messages you send us

When you use *Contact us* (in the app or on our support page), we receive your **name, e-mail address and question**, and the app version. We use them only to answer you and to improve the app. We keep them for as long as needed to help you, and delete them on request. We never sell them or use them for marketing.

## 5. Purchases

BitClock Pro is bought through **Google Play** or the **Apple App Store**. They handle the payment and your payment details; we never see them. The app only receives whether you have an active subscription, from the store, on your phone. The stores' own privacy policies apply to the purchase.

## 6. Watches and bands

With *Watch & band* (on unless you switch it off), BitClock shows an ordinary notification that your phone's own watch or band app mirrors to your wrist. BitClock itself sends nothing to the watch or anywhere else.

## 7. What we don't do

- We don't create accounts or profiles.
- We don't use analytics, crash-reporting or advertising services.
- We don't track you across apps or websites.
- We don't sell or share personal data.

## 8. Children

BitClock is not made for children under 13 and we don't knowingly collect data from them. The optional *dark jokes* are for adults (18+) and ask for confirmation before they are switched on.

## 9. Your rights

You can ask us for a copy of the personal data we have about you (in practice, only *Contact us* messages), to correct it, or to delete it: write to us with **Contact us**, in the app or on our [support page](./). If you live in the European Union, you have these rights under the GDPR, and you can complain to your data protection authority. The legal basis for answering your message is your request itself (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR) and our legitimate interest in supporting the app (Art. 6(1)(f)).

## 10. Security

Every internet connection the app makes is encrypted (HTTPS). Data on your phone is protected by your phone's own security (screen lock, encryption).

## 11. Changes

If this policy changes, we update it here and change the date at the top. Important changes are also mentioned in the app's release notes.
